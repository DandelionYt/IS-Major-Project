The Role of ICT in Achieving SDGs in Papua New Guinea

We explore how ICT drives sustainable development across education, health, climate, and economy in Papua New Guinea.

Read More

Abstract

This report dives into the role of Information & Communication Technology (ICT) in achieving Sustainable Development Goals in Papua New Guinea, which is aligned to Papua New Guinea’s Vision 2050. ICT has become a major influencer in major sectors such as health, education, poverty and economics and climate and resilience. he research in this report states that ICT increases in education, by enhancing students with the skills of how to use ICT, enhancing communication between lecturers and students. In terms of healthcare, ICT provides telemedicine, health strategies, digital health systems that can easily be accessed by anyone especially for people living in rural areas. Economic wise, ICT improves efficiency, improves the growth of a business, the production of food (agricultural wise) and helping Papua New Guinea reduce the dependency on natural resources. And lastly, ICT helps the people by giving them the predictions of the climate, helps them be prepared for disasters, and etc. Coming with benefits, there are also barriers and challenges that the country faces in terms of ICT. This includes the geographical landscape of the country and of the communities in the country, the high costs of ICT materials, limitations to power in rural areas of the country, lack of digital skills and knowledge by individuals, not strong policies and rules, language barriers, not strong security. Because of the geographical landscape, this makes implementation in Papua New Guinea a highly costly thing. The recommendations provided in this report suggests making proper and stronger ICT infrastructure, creating a bigger and stronger range of network towers so that rural areas are not affected, switching to renewable energy, using stronger ICT policies. By fixing the barriers and challenges faced, Papua New Guinea would be able to achieve Vision 2050 at a faster rate which would create a sustainable community.

Introduction

Papua New Guinea has gained the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and small Island developing states accelerated modalities of action (SAMOA pathway) to achieve the country’s vision-2050. The Sustainable Development Goals were implemented into Medium-Term Development Plan III (2018-2022) with other national policies, legislative and budgets. Papua New Guinea also has its own Sustainable Development Goals for the country itself, called the “Vision 2050”. Vision 2050 is a national development plan/framework which was launched in 2009 on November. It sets a pathway for Papua New Guinea’s political, economic, environmental and social development until 2050. According to the vision 2050 of Papua New Guinea, the mission is “for the country to attain our dream to be smart, wise, fair, healthy and a happy society by 2050”, (Taskforce, 2009).
Below are for (4) major importance of ICT out of the many stated in the Vision 2050 in Papua New Guinea;

  • Improving Education
  • Improving Healthcare
  • Provides Opportunities & Economic Growth
  • Climate and Resilience
The purpose of this report is to examine how ICT achieves sustainable development goals in Papua New Guinea. It also examines how ICT improves education, healthcare, how it provides opportunities, how it helps economic growth, see how ICT improves accessibility in terms of buying and purchasing goods and services and how it enables communication across distances, enabling people from rural areas to communicate to people living in urban areas. And lastly to provide recommendations to further improve ICT in achieving sustainable goals in Papua New Guinea.

Overview of ICT

Papua New Guinea is making slow progress in developing ICT infrastructure, but there still lies a significant gap compared to regional and global records (averages). Referring to the “state of digital” in Papua New Guinea in 2025 which states “A total of 5.03 million cellular mobile connections were active in Papua New Guinea in early 2025, with this figure equivalent to 47.2 percent of the total population. However, some of these connections only includes services such as voice notes and messages, and some don’t include access to the internet. There are approximately 2.57 million users using the internet in PNG at the start of early this year, while online penetration stood at 24.1 percent. Papua New Guinea was home to 1.30 million social media user identities in January 2025, equating to 12.2 percent of the total population.”, (Kemp, 2025).In which most of the internet users come from people living in the urban areas of the country, while rural areas still remain unserved. Mobile access has become common in today’s generation, in PNG, digicel, telikom, vodafone have dominated and have a major impact and a major contributor of the telecommunications sector here in Papua New Guinea. Mobile penetration is high, however the coverage and quality varies dependent on the provinces in PNG. However, despite having this percentages, Papua New Guinea still faces ICT challenges due to the geographical landscape of the country. Because of the rough terrains and the scattered islands, this makes ICT infrastructure rollout very costly and difficult to build. In addition, the high prices of mobile data are unaffordable for the people living in rural areas, where income is low. Papua New Guinea has implemented policies and strategies to help build its evolution and also strengthen the role of ICT in Papua New Guinea. The Digital Government Act 2022 states that; “Being an act to – provide for digital government through the use of information and communication technologies; and enable the streamlining, planning, coordination, development and implementing ICT across the whole of government, which includes digital services, digital infrastructure, digital skills and all other aspects of digital government and for related purposes.”, (Technology, 2022).

Health

In simple terms is the wellbeing of a person’s body. But what has ICT had to do with it? ICT plays a major role in supporting health, by promoting health equality to everyone, improving healthcare making it accessible throughout the country. These acts play a major role in achieving SDGs especially related to health.

Figure 3: launching of the digital health strategy (2025 - 2030) and digital tool kit in Port Moresby.

Based on the National Digital health strategy 2025 – 2030, outlines an approach to implementing digital tools that will enable for connecting all parts of health systems, including health information exchange platforms and mobile data collection systems. Through the IMPACT Health project, digital dashboards have been trialed in 4 pilot provinces of Papua New Guinea. These provinces include, Central, Simbu, East Sepik and East New Britain. The project was purposely for helping health managers track pharmaceutical supplies, audit facilities and improve service delivery, especially in remote areas.

Figure 4: . launching of the new digital strategy, ICT policies, and digital health toolkit in June 2025

Therefore, by integrating ICT services in the health system, Papua New Guinea is making healthcare accessible to its population, building a more responsive and equitable health sector, accelerating progress towards the SDG targets and national goals outline in vision 2050.

Poverty

What is poverty? Poverty in simple terms means when a country has less amount of resources mainly basic needs. “In Papua New Guinea, about 40% of the population leave in poverty, with rural communities most affected.”, ((ADB), 2021). Listed below is how ICT improves Poverty so it achieves sustainable development.

  • Helps Access to Basic Needs – ICT makes people living in the rural areas of Papua New Guinea to have access to basic needs like water, education, healthcare and basic services.
  • Enhances Education Outcomes – ICT enhances education outcomes, meaning it improves the education result of students by providing them with digital platforms to help them with their studies, makes the student’s life easy meaning it makes the student take countless online courses at the same time.
  • Agriculture - “ICT comes with mobile and online platforms, which gives farmers access to marketing opportunities, and increasing income and food.”, (IFAD, 2023). See Figure 5.

Economy

What is economy? Economy refers to the system through which a country produces, distributes, and consume goods and services. According to research, “Papua New Guinea’s economy heavily depends on two key sectors, which are agriculture, forestry & fisheries.” (World bank, 2025). Listed below is how ICT has change and is changing the economy of Papua New Guinea;

  • Enhances Business Efficiency - “ICT increases efficiency which opens up new opportunities in the marketing sector. Businesses could use digital platforms to reach wider markets, while government agencies could adopt online systems to make services more transparent and accessible.”, (Department of ICT , 2023).
  • Supports Digital Transformation in Health and Agriculture – “Telemedicine enables rural health workers to consult specialist, improving healthcare. And in terms of agriculture, it provides digital platforms which help farmers access live markets to see the latest prices of the goods they produce, also ICT provides them with websites accessed by internet to provide them with good agricultural advices which boost agricultural productivity.”, (Department of ICT , 2023)
  • Promotes Economic Expansion – “ICT provides internet in Papua New Guinea which influences the development of industries such as E-commerce, digital industries and services (financial services) which reduces reliability on natural resources.”, (Oxford Business Group, 2019)
  • Increases delivery of services – “ ICT provides online system streamline public services, which makes government operations more accessible, more transparent so that it is easily accessed by the people.”, (Department of ICT , 2023)
  • Helps sustainable growth – “Making more and more digital infrastructure helps improve the economy, which makes it more resilient and inclusive.”, (Oxford Business Group, 2019)
Barriers & Challenges

ICT in Papua New Guinea is not that easy to setup, and to run. There are barriers and challenges which affects the implementation of the role of ICT in achieving sustainable development goals. Listed below are some of them faced in Papua New Guinea.

1. Geographical Landscape

  • Due to the geographical landscape of the country, ICT has a major struggle. That is why not all places have network coverage, some places have telecommunication towers while others don’t.

2. High Costs

  • High cost of internet data by the service providers makes it difficult for everyone to afford good and reliable ICT services.
  • High cost of ICT infrastructure like computers and proper computing labs, makes it difficult for proper online learning.
  • High cost of ICT tech makes it expansive for telecommunication towers to be placed everywhere in the country.
  • In conclusion, the high costs of ICT infrastructure and services makes it harder for not only the government but the people as well.

3. Limited Electricity

  • Almost 3 quarters of the population of Papua New Guinea is located in the rural areas where electricity affects the people living there. Most of ICT’s infrastructure needs electricity to run.
  • An example of this that; Some hospitals in rural areas use telemedicine which run on generators, so they reduce the consumption of fuel to save up so that the generator can last longer meaning they will have power longer.

4. Digital Divide

  • Most people living in urban areas have proper ICT infrastructure and have proper access than the people living in the urban areas.
  • Most elders in PNG do don’t understand and see the benefits of ICT and in turn refuse to use it or stop the younger ones from using it make it harder for the them to use it freely and take advantage of its benefits.

5. Low Digital Skills & Knowledge

  • Many teachers currently teaching in schools struggle to use and teach ICT properly as it is a newly introduced concept in Papua New Guinea
  • Because of low digital knowledge, most business in PNG are unable to reach the wider market using online platforms.

6. Policy & Regulation

  • Because ICT is a new concept there are no proper rules and guidelines to guide the people on how to use ICT ethically.
  • People are at risk of personal data exploitation.

7. Limited Investment & Funding

  • Not enough funding is being allocated in developing ICT services in the country
  • Due to lack to the geographical location of Papua New Guinea, there government does not have enough funds to cater for establishing more ICT infrastructure in the country (caters for both the location of the country and the location of the communities and where they are located in the country, especially the rural areas).

8. Culture & Language

  • Papua New Guinea, home to over 850 languages with English seen as a foreign language makes it harder for people when using ICT. For example; A highlander calls his coastal lecturer. The difference between the languages between this two people would lead to a communication barrier.
  • Forests are seen as the treasures of our villagers, clearing them to build ICT buildings would make the people in the village have a repulsive response towards ICT.

9. Security & Privacy

  • Forests are seen as the treasures of our villagers, clearing them to build ICT buildings would make the people in the village have a repulsive response towards ICT.
  • People are at risk of being hacked.

10. Environmental & Disaster Risks

  • Papua New Guinea is prone to earthquake and landslides which can damage ICT infrastructure like underground data cables and telecommunication towers.

Conclusion

Recommendations

Listed below are some recommendations to improve the role of ICT in achieving Sustainable Development Goals in Papua New Guinea.

  • Make stronger telecommunication towers which have a strong connection. The stronger the connection the better the quality of the network. For example; if the network connectivity is strengthened, the better the quality of the call, video call would be.
  • Improve the network range of the ICT infrastructure (telecommunications tower) which would allow people to connect to the network even when they are distances away from the tower. This would fix the first challenge stated above “Geographical landscape”.
  • Increase the income per hour of the citizens of Papua New Guinea. This would not only fix the high cost issues but also make the people afford basic wants for ICT infrastructure. For example; If the person is a computer student, he can afford laptop wants like a mouse, keyboard and etc.
  • Use renewable energy to produce power especially for the people living in rural areas of the country. Instead of using generators which needs fuel, which would cost another expense in buying it. The government should invest in renewable energy (power). Not only would renewable energy improve the fuel issue but it would also provide power for all the people living in the rural areas of Papua New Guinea.
  • Government should concentrate mainly on developing and improving the rural parts of Papua New Guinea first before continuing to improving the urban areas of the country. When the rural areas are improved, this would allow ICT services to be easily implemented.
  • Implement ICT based programs so that every person has a basic knowledge on ICT. For example; the ICT program that DWU offered in Semester One (1).
  • Government should implement stronger and effective ICT policies. Not only stronger policies but the government should be effective software’s that protects sensitive data such as passwords, etc.
  • Implement local language translators for the people of Papua New Guinea. So that the people can understand what the other party is saying even if they are from different cultures.
  • The ICT buildings the government builds should have a stronger base foundation which would withstand anything nature throws at them.
  • And lastly the government should make wise decisions on ICT so that, not only would ICT impact the people but it would also play a major role in improving the country

Conclusion

In conclusion, ICT has a major role in achieving sustainable development goals in Papua New Guinea especially on major sectors such as Education, Health, Poverty and Economics, and Climate and Resilience. Although there are challenges such as geographical factors, high costs, ICT skills, policies and etc., which can affect ICT in the country, taking in solutions like, making better infrastructure, using proper software, implementing better ICT policies, and the government making wise decisions, should be able to fully use the full potential of ICT in developing the country and not only on achieving SDGs. So Papua New Guinea can progress at a faster rate in terms of sustainable development, block exiting loop holes related to ICT, and create a better future for the people.
